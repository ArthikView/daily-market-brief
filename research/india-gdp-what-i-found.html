India’s GDP: what I found

India’s growth story has two layers: strong real economic expansion, and a more complicated debate over the size of the economy when measured in US dollars.

BY CHANDAN PATHAK [ ECONOMIST ] · RESEARCH NOTES AS OF 21 NOVEMBER 2026
← BACK TO COVERAGE

India remains one of the fastest-growing large economies. But real growth, nominal GDP in rupees and GDP converted into dollars answer different questions. Treating them as interchangeable is where much of the ranking confusion begins.

01 / Growth is strong, but the pace is easing

Real GDP growth was reported at 7.4% in Q4 FY25, 7.8% in Q1 FY26 and 8.2% in Q2 FY26. The Q1 FY27 reading was 7.8%. The broad picture is still one of rapid expansion, although the pace is below the 8.2% reading cited for Q2 FY26.

The 7.2%–7.8% forecast spread is informative in its own right. The institutions broadly expect growth to remain fast, but differ on how much agricultural weakness, inflation and other headwinds may weigh on the quarter.

Reading the data: A forecast is not a provisional estimate, and a provisional estimate is not a final estimate. The Q2 FY27 figures above must not be quoted as actual GDP growth.

02 / How large is the economy? The sources disagree

Dollar-denominated GDP rankings can change even when real output continues to expand. The conversion from rupees to dollars, revisions to nominal GDP and the reference year all matter.

Source / referenceClaim cited in this research
Government year-end review · December 2025About $4.18 trillion, with India described as having overtaken Japan as the fourth-largest economy.
GDP base-year revision · 2026FY26 nominal GDP revised from ₹357 lakh crore to ₹345.5 lakh crore, equivalent to roughly $3.8 trillion at the conversion used in the research.
IMF · April 2026 WEOAbout $4.15 trillion, with India ranked sixth behind Japan ($4.38 trillion) and the UK ($4.26 trillion).
IMF projections citedAbout $4.58 trillion in 2027 and $6.79 trillion by 2031, implying a return to fourth place in 2027 and a possible third-place position by 2031.

The apparent ranking reversal is largely a nominal-measurement and currency-conversion story in the figures assembled here. A lower revised nominal base and rupee depreciation can reduce the dollar value of GDP even when real domestic production has not contracted.

Purchasing-power-parity (PPP) comparisons tell a different story. The research source set places India third, at roughly $14 trillion, behind China and the United States. PPP adjusts for differences in domestic price levels; it is not interchangeable with market-exchange-rate GDP.

Important comparison rule: Do not compare a government statement from one date with an IMF estimate from another as if they were the same statistical vintage. Check the reference year, exchange-rate assumption, revision status and whether the measure is nominal GDP or PPP GDP.

03 / FY27 outlook

The cited outlooks point to continued expansion around the 7% range, with some moderation risks during the second half of the year.

  • Economic Survey 2025–26: real growth range of 6.8%–7.2%, with potential growth near 7%.
  • ICRA: full-year growth estimate of 7.1%, with an expected second-half slowdown.
  • ADB and Moody’s: estimates around 7.0% in the source set.
  • Government ambition: a nominal GDP size of about $7.3 trillion by 2030.

These are estimates, forecasts or policy ambitions from different institutions, not a single consensus projection. Their assumptions and publication dates should be checked before direct comparison.

04 / Risks and supporting indicators

Risks being flagged

  • Deficient rainfall and El Niño-related weather risks affecting agricultural output.
  • Higher inflation and input costs putting pressure on household purchasing power and business margins.
  • Energy-price volatility and geopolitical tensions, including developments in the Middle East and US tariff policy.
  • A high base effect following the previous year’s GST rate rationalisation.

Supporting signs cited

  • Gross GST collections grew by about 15% during July–September, according to the research notes.
  • Industrial production rose 8.0% year on year in August, as cited in the source set.

These indicators offer useful context, but neither one alone establishes the direction of GDP. Tax collections can reflect prices, compliance and timing; industrial production is one part of the wider economy.

WHAT I TAKE AWAY

The growth story and the size story are not the same story.

Real growth around 7% or higher remains a strong headline. But claims about India’s global economic size depend on the GDP series, revision vintage, exchange rate and ranking source. Always ask: Is this real or nominal? Is it measured in rupees, dollars or PPP terms? Is it an actual estimate or a projection?
