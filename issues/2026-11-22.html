TEN-DAY RESEARCH DIGEST

India & Global Markets:
What moved the story?

A source-led review of the key market, policy and macroeconomic developments from 13 to 22 November 2026. The window includes the Friday 13 November close and the five Indian trading sessions from 16–20 November. Saturday 21 and Sunday 22 November were weekends, not regular cash-market sessions.

13–22 NOVEMBER 2026RESEARCH CUT-OFF · 22 NOV 2026BY CHANDAN PATHAK [ ECONOMIST ]
← THE DAILY MARKET BRIEF HOME
Executive read: Indian benchmarks extended their advance for a fourth consecutive week and the rupee strengthened, while wholesale-price inflation accelerated sharply and US technology-led selling intensified. The central tension was a strong domestic market against a more unsettled global risk backdrop.

1. Market dashboard: India

Reported facts. Between the 13 November close and 20 November close, the Nifty 50 rose from 25,144.30 to 25,800.60, a gain of about 2.61%. The Sensex advanced from 81,592.45 to 83,919.71, about 2.85%. On 20 November alone, the Nifty gained 1.12% and the Sensex 1.08%. The market completed a fourth consecutive positive week. Business Standard's 20 November market report provides the closing figures and the reported drivers.

Sector and breadth context. Banks were a major source of support, with the Bank Nifty outperforming the headline indices during the week. However, broader participation was less uniform: a weekly market review reported gains in the Nifty 500 and mid-caps, but a decline in its small-cap measure. This divergence matters because a benchmark rally can look healthier than the average listed stock's experience. See the 16–20 November weekly market review for sector and breadth detail.

Interpretation. The rise in large-cap indices alongside weaker small-cap breadth suggests leadership was concentrated rather than evenly distributed. Bank-led strength is constructive for index performance, but it should not be mistaken for a broad-based advance across every segment.

2. Rupee, foreign flows and crude

Reported facts. Reuters reported that the rupee strengthened past ₹93 per US dollar intraday on 20 November and finished the session at ₹92.67 per dollar. The report attributed the move to portfolio inflows into Indian equities, lower oil prices, reduced spot-dollar demand following RBI measures, and the unwinding of bearish offshore rupee positions. Reuters also reported record foreign buying in Indian shares during November up to that date. Read the Reuters currency report.

Interpretation. A stronger rupee can ease imported-cost pressure, especially for energy and other dollar-priced inputs. But the durability of the move depends on whether portfolio inflows persist, crude remains contained, and global dollar demand moderates. A short-lived currency rally should not be treated as a permanent change in India's external position.

3. Inflation watch: wholesale prices accelerated

Official release, 16 November. The Ministry of Commerce and Industry reported provisional October 2026 wholesale-price inflation of 12.29% year on year, up from 11.39% in September. The fuel-and-power group recorded 32.84% inflation, compared with 25.85% in September. Primary articles inflation was 9.24%, while manufactured-products inflation was 9.54%. The ministry identified mineral oils and petroleum products, crude petroleum and natural gas, electricity, food articles and several manufactured categories among the contributors. Official October WPI release (PIB).

Interpretation. The acceleration in WPI, particularly in fuel and power, raises a cost-pressure question for producers and businesses. It does not automatically mean an equivalent increase in consumer inflation: WPI and CPI measure different baskets and price stages. The key issue is whether higher input costs are absorbed in margins or passed through to retail prices over subsequent months.

4. External accounts: RBI's September balance-of-payments update

Official release, 13 November. The RBI's preliminary September 2026 data showed a current-account deficit of US$7.4 billion, compared with US$5.2 billion in September 2025. For April–September 2026, the current-account deficit was US$25.8 billion, compared with US$18.0 billion in the corresponding period a year earlier. In September, the merchandise trade deficit was US$30.0 billion, while the net services surplus was US$19.2 billion. The overall balance recorded a surplus of US$8.8 billion for September. RBI: September 2026 balance of payments.

Interpretation. The wider year-on-year current-account deficit is a reminder that India's external balance remains sensitive to merchandise imports and global energy prices. Services exports and capital/financial flows can provide offsets, but the current account and the overall balance are different measures. The recent rupee recovery should therefore be assessed alongside the underlying trade and income flows, not in isolation.

5. Global markets: technology risk meets rate uncertainty

Reported facts. Reuters reported a sharp US equity decline on 16 November as investors reassessed risks in the artificial-intelligence trade ahead of Nvidia's results and Anthropic's planned market debut. On 20 November, the Associated Press reported another steep technology-led sell-off: the S&P 500 fell 3.7% that day, the Nasdaq fell 5.3%, and the S&P 500's weekly decline was its steepest in more than 18 months. Reuters, 16 November · Associated Press, 20 November.

Interpretation for India. A global technology sell-off can affect Indian IT sentiment, foreign portfolio allocation and risk appetite even when domestic fundamentals are different. India's relative resilience during this period is notable, but it does not eliminate the possibility of spillovers through global funds, currency markets or valuation repricing.

6. RBI and financial-system developments

  • 13 November: RBI published September 2026 balance-of-payments data, providing an updated view of the current account, services trade and capital flows. RBI release.
  • 19 November: RBI released October 2026 overseas direct investment data. The release confirms publication of the monthly dataset; this digest does not infer a trend from the announcement alone. RBI release.
  • 20 November: RBI issued the Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) (Second Amendment) Regulations, 2026. Readers should consult the regulation itself for the precise compliance provisions. RBI foreign-exchange press releases.

8. Watchlist for the next sessions

  • Whether Nifty and Sensex can hold their recent gains with broader market participation.
  • Whether foreign portfolio flows remain supportive and whether the rupee's strength persists.
  • Oil-price direction and its interaction with India's import bill and inflation outlook.
  • Further evidence of wholesale-cost pass-through into consumer prices and corporate margins.
  • US technology-sector volatility, global bond yields and changing expectations for Federal Reserve policy.
  • RBI's subsequent statistical releases and any clarification on the foreign-exchange regulatory amendments.

Research notes and source discipline

This is a ten-calendar-day digest, not ten separate daily trading reports. Indian cash-market index performance is summarised for the 13 November starting close and the five sessions from 16–20 November. No regular Indian cash-equity session took place on 21–22 November. Figures are reported as published by the cited sources; where sources differ in methodology or timing, the underlying publication should be checked before making comparisons.

Editorial distinction: “Reported facts” summarise published releases or market reports. “Interpretation” is the author's analysis and is not a forecast. This publication is educational and informational, not investment advice or a recommendation to trade.
