India macro: official review puts supply-side inflation risks back in focus

REPORTED FACTS · 28 NOVEMBER

Reuters reported on Saturday that the government's monthly economic report sees a possibility of inflation picking up in coming months because of weather and supply-related uncertainties. The report notes that festivities extending into November and the start of the wedding season could raise costs of food and non-food items. It also flags Middle East conflict as a source of volatility for oil import bills and maritime freight rates, and global trade-policy uncertainty as a risk to the external sector. Domestic demand and industrial activity remain growth supports. Read Reuters' report, 28 November 2026. The Department of Economic Affairs maintains the official Monthly Economic Review archive.

WHY IT MATTERS · ANALYSIS

The message is a two-sided one: domestic demand and industrial activity are supporting output, but the near-term inflation path remains exposed to food availability, weather and logistics. A supply-driven price increase can complicate monetary policy because higher prices do not necessarily signal stronger demand. The RBI will need to distinguish persistent broad-based inflation from temporary or category-specific supply shocks. The report is a risk assessment, not a new CPI print or a quantified inflation forecast.

Reuters also noted that October retail inflation was 6.09% year-on-year, above the RBI's 6% upper tolerance level under the new CPI series. That is the reported October reading, not a November estimate. See the 28 November Reuters report for the context.

The week's reversal came alongside a weaker rupee and elevated crude prices. Rupee, oil and external balance

REPORTED FACTS

Reuters reported that the rupee closed Friday at ₹92.99 per dollar, down 0.6% on the session and 0.3% over the week. The report said foreign investors had bought about $16 billion of Indian equities in November, helping support the currency, while rising oil prices and renewed demand for AI-heavy markets presented risks to that support. Reuters, 27 November 2026.

INDIA TRANSMISSION · ANALYSIS

For an oil-importing economy, a higher dollar crude price and a weaker rupee can compound the domestic-currency cost of energy imports. The pass-through to retail prices depends on taxes, pricing decisions, inventories, hedging and the duration of the move. Foreign portfolio flows can cushion currency pressure, but they are market-sensitive and should not be treated as a guaranteed offset.

Friday's oil settlement and Sunday's OPEC+ decision

The Wall Street Journal's Friday settlement report put Brent at $95.44 per barrel, down 1.2% on the day but up 3.1% for the week; WTI settled at $83.08, down 0.7%. The US session was shortened for the day after Thanksgiving. These are Friday settlement values, not Saturday quotes. WSJ/Dow Jones, 27 November 2026.

Reuters reported that OPEC+ is likely to roll over current output targets for at least the first quarter of 2027 amid continuing disruption to Middle East exports and disagreements over members' capacity assessments. The report explicitly said no decision had yet been reached. Ministers are scheduled to meet on Sunday. Reuters, 26 November 2026.

WHAT TO WATCH

The market's reaction will depend not only on the headline production target but also on signals about compliance, spare capacity and the timetable for resolving quota disagreements. For India, the relevant transmission is the landed cost of crude, freight and insurance, rather than the Brent screen price alone.

RBI: reserve-management flexibility, not a policy-rate cut

OFFICIAL RBI ACTION · 27 NOVEMBER

The RBI reduced the minimum daily maintenance of CRR from 99% of the requirement to 90%, effective from the fortnight beginning 1 December 2026. The circular cites its assessment of the current and evolving macroeconomic situation and liquidity conditions. Read RBI notification RBI/2026-27/310.

INTERPRETATION

This changes the daily maintenance floor within the reporting fortnight; it does not reduce the prescribed CRR ratio itself and is not a repo-rate decision. It gives banks more flexibility in managing reserve balances day by day, while the average CRR requirement remains applicable. The practical test will be whether overnight funding conditions become smoother after the change takes effect.

Economist's perspective

The weekend's central theme is the tension between resilient domestic activity and a more complicated near-term inflation outlook. The government review's emphasis on weather, food supply and freight is important because these pressures can emerge even when demand is not overheating. Meanwhile, the rupee-oil combination keeps the external-cost channel in focus. Monday's IIP and GDP releases will help assess the growth side of the balance; Sunday's OPEC+ meeting may add information about the energy-cost side. Neither should be interpreted in isolation.
