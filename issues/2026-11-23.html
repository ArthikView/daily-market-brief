DAILY EDITION · MONDAY · 23 NOVEMBER 2026

A sharp market reversal, but India's activity pulse stays expansionary

Indian equities gave back their early gains and closed sharply lower as investors booked profits in banks, financials and metal stocks. The November flash PMI continued to signal private-sector expansion, while the RBI conducted two variable-rate repo operations to provide short-term liquidity.

The day in 60 seconds

  • Equities: Sensex lost 1.77% and Nifty 50 fell 1.75%, reversing a positive opening after a four-week rally.
  • Sector leadership: Banks, financials and metals were the main sources of pressure. IT and consumer durables were among the few sectors to finish higher.
  • Economic activity: November's flash composite PMI edged up to 56.3. Manufacturing strengthened to a nine-month high of 56.0, while services eased to 55.3. All remained above the 50 expansion threshold.
  • RBI liquidity: The RBI reported results for two VRR auctions, each with a notified amount of ₹50,000 crore and allotments close to the notified amount.
  • Global picture: Asian risk sentiment remained fragile after the technology-led sell-off, while US equity futures and European shares rebounded during Monday's trading.

1. Indian markets: opening optimism fades

The domestic market opened higher but could not hold the gains. The Nifty 50 ended at 25,349.65, down 450.95 points, while the Sensex closed at 82,435.99, lower by 1,483.72 points. The reversal came after a strong four-week advance and amid positioning ahead of the weekly derivatives expiry and Tuesday's market holiday.

2. India PMI: manufacturing improves, services cool

The headline: expansion continues

India's private-sector activity remained in expansion territory in November. The HSBC Flash Composite PMI came in at 56.3, marginally above October's 56.2 and in line with the consensus estimate of 56.3. The more interesting movement was within the two major sectors: manufacturing strengthened noticeably, while services lost some momentum.

1. Manufacturing: the strongest positive surprise

WHAT THE DATA SHOW

The Flash Manufacturing PMI rose to 56.0 from 54.7. It exceeded the consensus estimate of 54.5 and the forecast of 54.4. Among the three reported indicators, manufacturing recorded the clearest month-on-month improvement and the largest positive gap against expectations.

WHY IT MATTERS · ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION

A stronger manufacturing PMI is a constructive signal for the goods-producing side of the economy. If sustained, stronger factory conditions can support industrial output, supply-chain activity, capacity utilisation and demand for business inputs. It is an encouraging leading indicator, not a direct measurement of industrial production or GDP growth.

The next step is to check the detailed PMI components and subsequent hard data, including the Index of Industrial Production (IIP), for confirmation. The headline alone does not tell us which component drove the improvement.

2. Services: still expanding, but momentum eased

WHAT THE DATA SHOW

The Flash Services PMI eased to 55.3 from 55.8. It was below the consensus estimate of 56.0 and the forecast of 55.5. The reading nevertheless remains comfortably above the 50 expansion threshold.

WHY IT MATTERS · ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION

Services activity continues to expand, but the softer reading suggests that the pace of improvement moderated compared with the previous month. Because services make up a substantial part of India's economic activity, the direction of this index matters for the overall growth picture.

One softer month should not be treated as evidence of a downturn. The useful question is whether the moderation persists in the coming releases and whether it appears across business activity, new business and employment components when the detailed report becomes available.

3. Composite PMI: a steady headline, a changing mix

The Composite PMI edged up from 56.2 to 56.3, exactly matching consensus and exceeding the stated forecast of 55.7. This points to continued expansion across the private sector. However, the near-flat composite movement conceals a divergence: manufacturing improved while services moderated.

That distinction is important. A single headline can summarise the direction of activity, but sector-level readings help identify where momentum is strengthening and where it is cooling. November's release is positive overall, but not a uniformly stronger reading across the economy.

3. RBI liquidity operations: two VRR auctions

The RBI published results for two variable-rate repo auctions conducted on 23 November. A repo operation provides short-term liquidity to the banking system against eligible collateral. The results show substantial demand for the funds offered.

WHY IT MATTERS

These operations are short-term liquidity-management tools. They should not be confused with a change in the policy repo rate or a new monetary-policy stance. Their tenor and reversal dates matter when assessing near-term liquidity conditions and money-market rates.

5. Global markets: futures rebound, risk appetite remains fragile

Reuters reported that Asian equities remained under pressure after the previous week's technology-led sell-off. South Korea's technology-heavy market was particularly weak, while Japanese markets were closed for a holiday. European shares advanced during Monday's session, led by technology and semiconductor stocks.

US equity futures also rebounded. Reuters reported that, at 7:01 AM ET, Dow futures were up 2.01%, S&P 500 futures 2.77% and Nasdaq-100 futures 3.43%. These are futures snapshots, not closing returns for the US cash indices. Investors remained focused on whether the spending commitments behind the AI investment cycle can generate sufficient future earnings.

GLOBAL TRANSMISSION TO INDIA

A recovery in global technology shares can improve sentiment toward risk assets, but it does not automatically remove valuation or earnings concerns. For India, the relevant channels include foreign portfolio flows, the dollar, global bond yields, commodity prices and the relative performance of domestic sectors.

Economist's perspective

Monday delivered two different signals. The equity market experienced a sharp reversal after a strong run, led by heavyweight banks and metals. At the same time, the November PMI suggested that private-sector activity continued to expand, with manufacturing gaining momentum and services slowing modestly. The RBI's two VRR operations added a separate liquidity-management dimension to the day's macro picture.

The balanced conclusion is not that the market decline proves the economy is weakening, nor that a positive PMI guarantees stronger share prices. Equity valuations and positioning can adjust faster than survey indicators. The next useful evidence will come from market breadth after the holiday, foreign-flow data, the rupee, global risk sentiment and confirmation from India's hard economic releases.

7. What does this mean for India's macro outlook?

  • Growth: All three PMI readings are above 50, consistent with ongoing private-sector expansion. The manufacturing improvement is a positive near-term signal.
  • Industrial activity: The factory reading provides a reason to watch upcoming IIP data for confirmation. PMI and IIP measure activity differently and should not be treated as interchangeable.
  • Services momentum: The moderation is worth monitoring because services remain a major part of India's output and employment landscape.
  • Inflation: The headline PMI values supplied here do not establish whether input-cost or output-price pressures increased or decreased. The prices components of the detailed survey are needed before drawing an inflation conclusion.
  • Monetary policy: This release alone is not enough to infer an RBI rate decision. Policymakers will assess a wider set of indicators, including inflation, growth, liquidity, credit and external conditions.

5. What markets may watch next

For investors and macro watchers, the release offers a constructive activity signal, especially from manufacturing. But the market impact will depend on how the PMI surprise interacts with other data and current valuations. The key follow-through indicators are:

  • Final November PMI readings and the detailed survey components.
  • Upcoming IIP data to test whether the manufacturing improvement is visible in measured industrial output.
  • Core and headline CPI inflation, especially alongside input-cost and selling-price trends.
  • Corporate results and management commentary for evidence of demand, pricing power and capacity utilisation.
  • RBI communication and liquidity conditions, without treating one PMI release as a policy signal.

Economist's perspective

November's flash PMI is a positive reading for India's near-term activity cycle. Manufacturing has delivered a meaningful improvement and the composite index remains stable in expansion territory. Services, however, have softened and missed expectations. The right interpretation is therefore resilient expansion with a shift in sectoral momentum, rather than an across-the-board acceleration.

For a fuller macro assessment, the PMI should be read alongside industrial production, inflation, credit growth, consumption indicators and the RBI's policy assessment. Leading indicators are valuable because they arrive early; they become more powerful when confirmed by the hard data that follows.
