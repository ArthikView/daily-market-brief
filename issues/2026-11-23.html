DAILY EDITION · MONDAY · 23 NOVEMBER 2026

A sharp market reversal, but India's activity pulse stays expansionary

Indian equities surrendered their early gains and closed sharply lower after a four-week advance. Banks and metals led the retreat, while November's flash PMI showed stronger factory momentum alongside slower services growth. The RBI also conducted two short-tenor liquidity operations.

The day in 60 seconds

  • Equities: The Sensex fell 1.77% and the Nifty 50 lost 1.75%, reversing early gains after a four-week winning run. The Nifty Bank declined 3.17%.
  • Sector split: Metals and banks were among the weakest groups. Nifty Metal fell 3.68%, while Nifty IT gained 1.69%.
  • Activity: The HSBC Flash India Composite PMI edged up to 56.3. Manufacturing improved to 56.0, while services eased to 55.3. All three readings remained above the 50 expansion threshold.
  • RBI liquidity: Two variable-rate repo auctions each had a notified amount of ₹50,000 crore. The RBI allotted ₹50,005 crore in the four-day operation and ₹50,003 crore in the two-day operation.
  • Currency and oil: The rupee closed weaker at ₹92.91 per US dollar. Brent crude was lower in a Reuters snapshot at 6:26 PM IST.
  • Global markets: European shares and US equity futures rebounded, but the recovery followed a severe technology-led sell-off and was not yet evidence of a durable change in risk appetite.

The domestic market opened higher but selling gathered pace through the session. At the close, the Moneycontrol closing report and Reuters' market report agreed on the benchmark closes.

Reuters reported that 14 of the 16 major sector indices declined. Banks and financial shares had gained roughly 18% to 22.5% over the preceding four weeks, making profit-taking in these groups especially influential. Moneycontrol also reported a fall of more than ₹5 lakh crore in the market value of BSE-listed companies. The broader mid-cap and small-cap indices fell less than the headline benchmarks, according to the same closing report.

WHY THE SESSION MATTERS · ANALYSIS

The sharp fall is consistent with a position reset after a strong run, with weekly derivatives expiry and Tuesday's exchange holiday adding to the near-term focus on liquidity and risk reduction. That is a plausible explanation reported by market participants, not proof that the medium-term trend has changed. A single session cannot establish a lasting reversal.

A major steel-sector transaction

REPORTED FACTS

Reuters reported that Tata Steel agreed to acquire an initial 26.38% stake in APL Apollo Tubes for about ₹15,511 crore and launch an open offer for up to a further 26%. Separately, APL Apollo is to acquire Tata Steel's tubes business and its entire stake in Tata Steel Colors for up to ₹28,000 crore. APL Apollo shares fell 21.3% and Tata Steel fell about 8.5% in Monday's trade.

WHY IT MATTERS · ANALYSIS

The linked transactions could reshape the downstream steel-tubes business, but the financing burden, open-offer participation, regulatory approvals and execution timetable matter. The stock reaction shows that investors were weighing transaction funding and structure, not simply the strategic rationale. The deal should not be treated as the sole explanation for the wider metal-sector decline.

2. India activity: manufacturing strengthens, services cool

The HSBC Flash India Composite PMI Output Index rose to 56.3 in November from 56.2 in October. The manufacturing PMI reached a nine-month high, while the services business-activity index eased. The figures were reported by Business Standard and Moneycontrol. The survey is compiled by S&P Global for HSBC; these are preliminary flash readings.

A reading above 50 signals expansion from the previous month. These survey indices are not direct measures of GDP or industrial production.

Moneycontrol's report said total new business expanded at its fastest pace since June and new export orders increased at the fastest pace in four months. Hiring trends diverged: manufacturing employment grew at the fastest pace in seven months, while service providers reduced employment marginally for the first time in nearly a year. Input-cost inflation remained subdued, although selling prices rose somewhat faster than in October.

WHAT THE PMI DOES AND DOES NOT SAY · ANALYSIS

The release points to continued private-sector expansion with a stronger factory contribution. It does not establish that every industry is accelerating, nor does it replace hard output, inflation or employment data. The softer services reading matters because services are a large part of India's economy. The next test is whether industrial production, credit, consumption and subsequent survey releases confirm the improvement.

The strong bid amounts indicate substantial demand for short-term central-bank liquidity at the auction windows. The operations help manage temporary liquidity conditions around a holiday-shortened week. Auction allotments and cut-off rates should be read alongside money-market rates and subsequent RBI operations, not as a standalone signal of the next policy-rate decision.

4. Rupee, bonds and commodities

Rupee

ONSHORE MARKET CLOSE · 23 NOVEMBER

The rupee closed at ₹92.91 per US dollar, 0.26% weaker than Friday's close of ₹92.67, ending a five-session winning run. It had briefly strengthened to ₹92.44 earlier in Monday's session, its strongest level since April. The closing figure and market explanation were reported by Reuters and Moneycontrol.

Reuters cited increased dollar demand from large importers and weaker domestic equity sentiment as factors behind the reversal. The distinction between the early-session high and the closing level is important: the rupee did not retain its strongest intraday level.

Government bonds

LATEST REPORTED WEEKLY OBSERVATION

Reuters' Monday market note put the benchmark 10-year government bond yield at 7.1868%, 12 basis points lower over the previous week. This is the latest clearly dated yield observation available for this edition, not a verified Monday closing quote. Read the Reuters rates and flows report.

Bond prices and yields move in opposite directions. Lower yields can ease benchmark funding costs, but the direction of government borrowing costs also depends on inflation expectations, global rates, liquidity and demand at auctions.

Crude oil and gold

BRENT CRUDE · INTRADAY SNAPSHOT

At 12:56 GMT (6:26 PM IST), Reuters reported Brent at $91.25 per barrel, down 1.43%, and US WTI at $82.27, down 1.37%. The report attributed the decline mainly to increased Middle Eastern and other supplies, while attacks on shipping and energy infrastructure continued to support a geopolitical risk premium. This is a timestamped futures snapshot, not a final settlement. Reuters oil-market report, 23 November.

Intraday reports showed MCX December gold futures higher, but the snapshots differed by timestamp and contract feed. See the Upstox afternoon quote and the PTI report carried by Rediff. No single closing or settlement price is reproduced here. Readers should distinguish an MCX futures contract from a retail jewellery quote.

INDIA TRANSMISSION CHANNELS · ANALYSIS

Lower crude prices can reduce the import bill and ease imported inflation pressure, all else equal. A weaker rupee works in the opposite direction by raising the rupee cost of dollar-priced imports. For bonds, the combination of domestic liquidity, inflation expectations and US Treasury yields matters more than any one daily quote. These channels can pull in different directions, so the net effect needs to be assessed over more than one session.

GLOBAL TRANSMISSION TO INDIA · ANALYSIS

The rebound in US futures and European technology shares offered some relief after a difficult week, but it followed a sharp valuation reset in AI-linked stocks. For India, the channels to monitor are foreign portfolio flows, the dollar, global bond yields, commodity prices and the relative performance of domestic banks, metals and IT. A one-day overseas bounce does not by itself establish that global risk appetite has normalised.

6. What this means for India's macro picture

Monday produced two different signals. The equity market corrected sharply after a strong run, while the PMI remained consistent with continued private-sector expansion. Manufacturing improved, services growth moderated and the employment picture differed across the two sectors.

  • Growth: The PMI is a constructive near-term activity signal, particularly for manufacturing, but needs confirmation from hard data.
  • Inflation: Subdued input-cost growth in the survey is encouraging, but the modest pickup in selling-price inflation means the full prices detail still matters.
  • External balance: Lower crude is supportive for India's import bill, while the rupee's weaker close partially offsets that benefit.
  • Financial conditions: RBI liquidity operations, bond yields and global rates will influence money-market conditions and funding costs.
  • Markets: The concentration of selling in banks and metals should be watched alongside market breadth when trading resumes.
ECONOMIST'S PERSPECTIVE

The balanced reading is resilient expansion, not an across-the-board acceleration. The manufacturing survey strengthened enough to keep the composite index in expansion territory, but services moderated and hiring was uneven. The market decline should not be read as proof that economic activity has weakened, just as a positive PMI should not be treated as a guarantee of higher equity prices. The next useful evidence will come from industrial production, inflation, credit, consumption, foreign flows and the next market session.
