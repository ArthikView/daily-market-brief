DAILY EDITION · THURSDAY · 26 NOVEMBER 2026

IT-led gains lift Dalal Street, as oil and rupee volatility return

Indian benchmarks extended their recovery for a second session, with information technology and realty shares leading. The rupee weakened as oil prices firmed and regional currencies softened. Stronger US growth and sticky core inflation have sharpened attention on the Federal Reserve's December decision, while the US Thanksgiving holiday leaves global trading conditions thinner than usual.

The Nifty 50 gained 65.25 points to finish at 25,884.50, up 0.25%. The BSE Sensex rose 390.31 points to 84,328.59, up 0.46%. Reuters reported the same percentage changes and closing levels; PL Capital's post-market report independently recorded the Nifty close at 25,884.50 and the Sensex at 84,329 when rounded. PL Capital's sector table showed Nifty IT up 3.66%, realty up 3.27% and metals up 1.17%. Pharma added 0.45% and oil & gas 0.34%. The Nifty Bank slipped 0.26%, auto fell 0.69%, FMCG declined 0.94% and consumer durables lost 1.33%. Of the Nifty 50 constituents, 22 advanced and 28 declined; broader market breadth was positive, with 2,149 stocks rising and 1,906 falling in PL Capital's report.

HCL Technologies rose 6.42%, BSE Ltd gained 5.70%, TCS added 4.89% and Infosys advanced 4.69%, according to PL Capital. The report attributed a portion of the broader market's positive tone to technology leadership and resilient institutional demand.

WHY IT MATTERS · ANALYSIS

The headline indices rose even though more Nifty 50 constituents fell than rose. That divergence matters: index-level gains were concentrated in selected large technology and other heavyweight stocks rather than reflecting a uniform rally across the benchmark.

Reuters said the Nifty and Sensex had gained about 12.3% and 13.3% respectively in November through Thursday, putting them on track for their strongest monthly performance since April 2020. It also reported approximately $15.16 billion of foreign portfolio buying in Indian equities so far this month. Those are month-to-date figures, not one-day flows. A fast monthly advance can coexist with short-term consolidation risk; the next test is whether earnings breadth and domestic participation can sustain the move.

2. India macro and RBI: Treasury-bill auction settles

RBI PRIMARY RELEASE · RESULT PUBLISHED 25 NOVEMBER · SETTLEMENT 26 NOVEMBER

The RBI's Treasury-bill auction for 91-day, 182-day and 364-day securities settled on 26 November. The notified competitive auction amounts totalled ₹23,000 crore: ₹8,000 crore, ₹8,000 crore and ₹7,000 crore respectively. The RBI's official result reported cut-off yields of 5.8846% for 91-day bills, 6.5288% for 182-day bills and 6.6387% for 364-day bills.

Competitive bids accepted were ₹4,329.75 crore, ₹7,600 crore and ₹6,650 crore respectively. Non-competitive bids accepted were ₹12,300 crore, ₹400 crore and ₹620 crore. The RBI's auction notice specifies that eligible non-competitive allocations sit outside the notified amount. Read the RBI full auction result and the 20 November auction notice.

LIQUIDITY AND FUNDING · ANALYSIS

The bill cut-offs provide a current official reference for short-term government borrowing costs. They should not be confused with the RBI policy repo rate. The auction result is a funding-market observation, not a fresh monetary-policy decision.

For the longer benchmark, two market-data providers placed the Indian 10-year government yield around 7.21% on 26 November, compared with 7.169% in the previous session. These are indicative interbank/OTC market quotes, not an exchange-traded closing print. A firmer oil price can complicate the inflation outlook and influence bond-market term premia, while government borrowing supply and global yields also matter. The yield level is therefore a market signal, not proof of a change in the RBI's policy stance. See Investing.com historical quotes and Trading Economics' India 10-year series.

3. Rupee and external balance: oil interrupts the currency recovery

REPORTED FACTS · REUTERS · 26 NOVEMBER

Reuters reported that the rupee traded between 92.33 and 92.65 before closing 0.2% weaker at ₹92.4450 per US dollar. The report linked the decline to rising oil prices and weakness in regional currencies. It also said the one-year USD/INR forward premium reached its highest level since January 2022 and had risen by more than 70 basis points over ten days, reflecting increased hedging demand and changes in dollar liquidity.

Read Reuters' rupee and forward-premium report. PL Capital's currency table showed ₹92.4799 for one US dollar in its 3:30 PM IST market snapshot; because the two observations are not presented as the same fixing, this edition uses Reuters' reported onshore close and records the vendor difference.

INDIA TRANSMISSION · ANALYSIS

India imports a large share of its crude requirement. A sustained rise in the dollar price of oil can increase the import bill; if the rupee also weakens, the local-currency cost of energy and energy-linked inputs can rise further. The potential pass-through runs through fuel, freight, petrochemicals and production costs, although the timing and extent depend on domestic pricing, taxes, inventories and global price persistence.

The recent foreign-flow support for Indian assets is a counterweight, but it is not a guaranteed currency floor. Importers' hedging demand, exporters' dollar conversion and RBI liquidity management can all affect near-term FX conditions. The one-year forward premium is a hedging-cost indicator, not a direct forecast of the future spot rate.

4. Commodities: supply risk in oil, rate sensitivity in gold

REPORTED FACTS · TIMESTAMPED MARKET SNAPSHOTS

Reuters reported Brent crude futures at $94.52 a barrel, down 0.1%, at 07:58 GMT on Thursday, after Brent had risen 3.5% in the previous session. A separate same-day market report from the Wall Street Journal placed Brent near $94.43, broadly consistent with the Reuters snapshot. The reports linked the earlier rise to renewed supply concerns after attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and reports of mines affecting shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. The market was also weighing expectations of a future supply surplus. See the Reuters oil-market report carried by Euronext and the Wall Street Journal market update.

Spot gold was down 0.4% at $3,565.41 per troy ounce at 08:30 GMT, according to a Reuters report. PL Capital's later 26 November closing data showed spot gold at $3,562.38, down 0.74%. The close and earlier snapshot are not identical timestamps, but both place bullion near $3,563–$3,565 per ounce. Gold had briefly fallen below $3,500 during the previous session. Read Reuters on gold and rate expectations and PL Capital's commodity closing table.

WHAT THE COMMODITY SIGNALS MEAN · ANALYSIS

Oil is being pulled in two directions. Shipping and infrastructure risks around the Gulf add a geopolitical premium, while expectations of ample supply and inventory changes can limit rallies. For India, the near-term macro channel is the import bill and the rupee; the market should distinguish a brief headline-driven spike from a sustained change in physical supply.

Gold faces a different cross-current. Higher US real yields and a firmer dollar can weigh on a non-yielding asset, while geopolitical uncertainty and central-bank demand can offer support. The previous session's sharp decline and Thursday's stabilisation do not, by themselves, establish a durable trend reversal.

5. Global markets and central banks: Thanksgiving pause, but macro signals remain active

REPORTED FACTS · ASSOCIATED PRESS · 26 NOVEMBER

US cash equity markets were closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving. In early European trading, Germany's DAX was up 1.9%, France's CAC 40 rose 1.8% and Britain's FTSE 100 gained 1.6%. In Asia, Japan's Nikkei closed 0.9% higher, South Korea's Kospi recovered 0.3% after a 6.2% fall the previous day, Taiwan's Taiex rose 2.5%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 1.2% and Australia's S&P/ASX 200 gained 0.8%. AP said US equity futures were up 0.5% in its snapshot.

On Wednesday, before the holiday, the S&P 500 finished 0.3% lower, the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.6% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.3%. Nvidia fell 3.9% as concerns about the AI investment cycle persisted. These are prior-session closes, not Thursday US cash-market moves. See Associated Press' global market report.

US DATA · OFFICIAL BEA RELEASES · 25 NOVEMBER

The US Bureau of Economic Analysis revised Q3 2026 real GDP growth to an annual rate of 4.3% in its second estimate, from the advance estimate of 3.4%; Q2 growth was 2.2%. The BEA said the upward revision mainly reflected stronger investment and consumer spending, while exports declined and imports increased. The annualised rate is a seasonally adjusted annual rate, not a year-on-year growth rate.

In its October Personal Income and Outlays release, the BEA reported that the PCE price index rose 0.3% month on month and 3.8% year on year. The core PCE index, excluding food and energy, also rose 0.3% month on month and 3.3% year on year. These are official BEA figures, not market estimates. Read the Q3 GDP second estimate and October Personal Income and Outlays release.

GLOBAL READ-THROUGH · ANALYSIS

The US combination of stronger real activity and persistent core inflation can make the Federal Reserve's disinflation task more difficult. Reuters reported that markets had increased expectations of a December rate increase. That is market pricing, not a commitment or forecast issued by the Federal Reserve. Higher US yields can support the dollar and affect emerging-market currency and bond pricing; the direction and scale of any spillover depend on subsequent data and policy communication.

Reuters also reported that the US administration had proposed new levies on imports from 14 trading partners, including India, citing concerns about excess industrial capacity. This is a proposal, not a tariff measure confirmed as implemented in the cited report. The eventual product coverage, legal process and effective dates would determine the impact on Indian exporters. The global AI-related sell-off and rebound also matter for India's IT sector: a change in global risk appetite can move technology valuations even when Indian company fundamentals have not changed on the same day.

India implications: three channels to keep separate

  1. Equity earnings and sector breadth: the IT-led advance is constructive for the headline indices, but the Nifty's 22 advancers versus 28 decliners shows why index returns should not be treated as a proxy for every stock or sector. Watch whether gains broaden beyond a handful of large constituents.
  2. Oil, inflation and the rupee: a sustained oil-price increase combined with currency weakness can lift the rupee cost of imports and feed into transport and input costs. A single day's price movement is not enough to infer a change in India's inflation path.
  3. Bond yields and policy flexibility: a higher 10-year yield can reflect global rates, oil-related inflation risk, supply expectations or domestic demand for duration. The RBI's liquidity operations and T-bill auctions should be read separately from the policy repo rate.

Economist's perspective

Thursday's market action contains two different messages. Indian equities continued to recover, and the IT sector supplied the strongest leadership. At the same time, the rupee's pullback and renewed oil sensitivity show that India's external-price exposure has not disappeared. Global markets were firmer in Asia and Europe, but the US holiday reduced the amount of fresh cash-market information available from Wall Street.

The most useful next step is to track confirmation rather than extrapolate a single session: whether equity gains broaden, whether crude remains elevated beyond short-lived supply headlines, whether currency hedging costs stay high, and what the next Indian IIP and GDP releases say about domestic activity. No numerical market forecast is made in this edition.
