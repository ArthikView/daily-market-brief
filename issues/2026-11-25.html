DAILY EDITION · WEDNESDAY · 25 NOVEMBER 2026

Dalal Street rebounds sharply, but the global risk-off signal has not cleared

Indian equities recovered strongly after the holiday break: the Sensex gained 1.82%, the Nifty 50 rose 1.85% and the rupee reached an eight-month high. The RBI allotted funds through two overnight liquidity operations. Yet global equities remained under pressure from renewed US tariff concerns and doubts about the AI investment cycle, while US inflation and GDP data were still ahead at this edition's cut-off.

1. India markets: a sharp recovery, with clear sector winners and laggards

REPORTED FACTS · NSE / BSE CLOSE · 25 NOVEMBER

The Nifty 50 opened at 25,658.35, reached 25,951.65 and closed at 25,819.25, a gain of 469.60 points or 1.85%. The BSE Sensex settled at 83,938.28, up 1,502.29 points or 1.82%, after touching an intraday high of 84,498.21. The Nifty Bank closed at 63,703.25, up 1.79%. These closing levels were reported by EqMint's post-market report and independently by the PTI report carried by Rediff.

Sector performance was led by Nifty FMCG, up about 3.8%; pharma, up about 2.4%; and financial services, up about 2.2%. Metal shares fell about 2.0%, while media declined about 4.4%. The figures are consistent across the NSE-index-based sector snapshot from ThePip and EqMint's closing report; minor differences reflect snapshot timing and rounding.

Among the Sensex constituents, Hindustan Unilever, Trent, ICICI Bank, InterGlobe Aviation, HDFC Bank and Bajaj Finance were among the leading gainers. Tata Steel, Bharti Airtel and Titan ended lower, according to the PTI market wrap carried by Rediff.

WHY THE MOVE MATTERS · ANALYSIS

The rebound followed Monday's sharp decline and the Tuesday exchange holiday. It should therefore be read as a recovery session, not as proof that recent volatility has ended. Buying in consumer staples, financials and pharma supported the headline indices, while weakness in metals and media shows that the move was not uniform across the market.

Reuters linked the recovery to foreign portfolio inflows and softer oil. It reported record month-to-date foreign investment of $14.28 billion in Indian equities by 25 November. That is a Reuters-reported cumulative figure, not a one-day flow estimate. Foreign demand can support both equities and the rupee, but the same flow channel can reverse if global risk aversion intensifies.

LIQUIDITY READ-THROUGH · ANALYSIS

Bids exceeded the notified amount in both operations, indicating substantial demand for short-term funds at the auction windows. The operations are liquidity-management tools; they do not by themselves signal a change in the policy repo rate or a new monetary-policy stance. The weighted average rates are auction outcomes, not a substitute for the policy rate.

RBI scrutiny of foreign-exchange transactions

REUTERS REPORT · 25 NOVEMBER · BANKER SOURCES

Reuters reported that the RBI had stepped up checks on importers' foreign-exchange transactions to ensure that larger deals are not deliberately split into smaller transactions to avoid the Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve requirement. The framework, introduced on 10 October, requires banks to set aside cash equal to 20% of the value of foreign-exchange derivative contracts above $2 million; transactions below that threshold are exempt from the additional reserve cost.

Reuters cited CCIL data showing the average dollar-buying transaction size had fallen from $3.6 million to about $1.7 million, while the number of such transactions rose 17% even as overall volumes fell 46%. The RBI did not immediately respond to Reuters' request for comment. This is a reported supervisory development, not a separately published RBI announcement. Read Reuters' report.

The distinction matters for importers: the reserve rule can raise hedging costs on qualifying contracts, while the reported scrutiny signals that the exemption is not intended to be used to restructure a larger exposure artificially.

4. Rupee, crude and gold: the import-price channel improves, but snapshots differ

Rupee

ONSHORE USD/INR · REPORTED CLOSE · 25 NOVEMBER

Reuters reported that the rupee closed at ₹92.23 per US dollar, gaining 0.7% on the day after touching ₹92.1850, its strongest level in eight months. Reuters attributed the move to foreign equity inflows, softer crude and improved domestic dollar supply-demand conditions. It reported a 2.9% gain for the rupee in November to date. A separate report placed the close at ₹92.22, a one-paise difference. See Reuters and Financial Express.

Crude oil

BRENT FUTURES · REUTERS SNAPSHOT AT 10:35 GMT / 4:05 PM IST

Brent crude futures were down 0.5% at $90.96 a barrel, while US WTI was down 0.8% at $81.43. These are timestamped futures observations, not settlement prices. Reuters said the market was balancing the prospect of a global supply surplus against shipping and geopolitical risks around the Strait of Hormuz. The upcoming OPEC+ meetings on 29 November will address 2027 output policy. The earlier Asian snapshot from Business Standard put Brent at $90.97, broadly consistent at that earlier time. Sources: Reuters and Business Standard.

Gold

SPOT GOLD · REUTERS SNAPSHOT AT 11:22 GMT / 4:52 PM IST

Spot gold was down 0.8% at $3,524.36 per troy ounce. Reuters linked the weakness to a firmer US dollar and expectations of higher interest rates ahead of the US PCE release. A separate Financial Express update placed spot gold near $3,525 an ounce. These are international spot observations, not Indian retail bullion rates or MCX settlement prices. Sources: Reuters and Financial Express.

INDIA TRANSMISSION · ANALYSIS

For an oil-importing economy, a softer crude price and a stronger rupee can work in the same direction: they reduce the local-currency cost of imported energy, all else equal, and can ease pressure on the trade balance and fuel-related input costs. The benefit is conditional. A renewed supply shock could reverse the oil move, while a stronger rupee can reduce rupee conversion values for exporters. Gold's near-term direction is more sensitive to the dollar, real yields and the expected path of US rates than to India's equity rally alone.

5. Global markets: tariff risk and AI valuation concerns return

REUTERS GLOBAL SNAPSHOT · 25 NOVEMBER

Reuters reported that its gauge of global equities was down 0.5%, while Asia-Pacific shares excluding Japan were down 3.1%. Investors were weighing the possibility of new US import duties on goods from 16 trading partners, alongside renewed selling in technology shares and doubts about the returns on debt-funded AI investment. The tariff measures were described as expected or under consideration, not as a completed policy announcement. Read Reuters' global markets report.

At 6:01 AM US Eastern time (4:31 PM IST), Reuters reported Dow futures down 0.76%, S&P 500 futures down 1.44% and Nasdaq 100 futures down 2.38%. These were pre-market futures, not US cash-market closes. US cash trading had not started by this edition's cut-off. The US market was scheduled to close for Thanksgiving on Thursday, 26 November, and reopen for a shortened session on Friday. Source: Reuters' US futures report.

European shares were mixed. Reuters reported the STOXX 600 up 0.21% to 634.1 points at 09:34 GMT (3:04 PM IST), with autos outperforming and technology lagging; a later global-market wrap described Europe as having surrendered its early gains. These are different snapshots, not a closing return. Reuters' Europe report provides the timestamped sector detail.

GERMANY · OFFICIAL GDP REVISION · 25 NOVEMBER

Germany's Federal Statistical Office (Destatis) revised third-quarter GDP growth to 0.1% quarter on quarter, seasonally and calendar adjusted, from the earlier 0.2% flash estimate. GDP was 1.2% higher year on year in price-adjusted terms. Destatis cited weaker-than-assumed September exports and retail turnover. This is a modest growth reading, not a contraction. Primary source: Destatis, press release 409, 25 November 2026.

GLOBAL READ-THROUGH · ANALYSIS

The global backdrop is not confirming a clean risk-on turn. India's rally and currency strength are notable, but the broader external setting still contains tariff uncertainty, AI-sector repricing and a potentially hawkish US rate path. For India, this matters through foreign portfolio flows, the dollar, US Treasury yields and demand for technology exports. Germany's softer quarterly growth also illustrates that global demand conditions remain uneven.

Economist's perspective

Today's Indian rebound has three supports that reinforce one another: foreign portfolio demand, a stronger rupee and lower oil prices. Those supports can improve near-term sentiment and reduce some imported-cost pressure. But they sit beside a less comfortable global picture: tariff uncertainty, renewed weakness in AI-linked shares and a US inflation release that could influence expectations for the Federal Reserve.

The key distinction for the next session is between a domestic recovery and a durable improvement in global risk appetite. The former is visible in today's closing indices. The latter still needs confirmation from overseas markets, currency flows and the response to US data. No numerical market forecast is warranted from this evidence alone.
